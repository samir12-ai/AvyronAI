Hi,

I reviewed the final Phase 4-A audit results carefully, and I think the outcome is actually very encouraging.

The key point is this:

The system did NOT fail at reasoning integrity.

What failed was domain-style adaptation.

That distinction matters a lot.

The deterministic infrastructure, integrity gates, evidence validation, canonical truth enforcement, phantom-evidence protections, and safety floor all behaved correctly during the treatment runs. That is the hardest architectural problem in LLM integration, and it is already solved successfully.

The reason the scores varied across industries is not because the LLM became unreliable or strategically weak. In fact, the dtc_ecom improvement (+7) strongly suggests the opposite: the commercial reasoning layer is capable of producing meaningfully deeper strategic interpretation when the domain language matches the seeded reasoning style.

The issue is that the current template corpus is heavily biased toward SaaS/operator vocabulary, so when the system entered local_services contexts, it carried over abstract startup-style phrasing into industries that require grounded, human, practical language.

This is a tuning and conditioning problem, not a doctrine failure.

In other words:

* the LLM is thinking correctly,
* but sometimes speaking in the wrong commercial dialect.

That is a far easier class of problem to solve than hallucination, broken truth systems, unstable orchestration, or uncontrolled reasoning overrides.

Most importantly:
the treatment runs proved that the adaptive reasoning layer can improve strategic depth without breaking deterministic reliability boundaries.

That was the actual existential risk of this phase, and the system handled it successfully.

My recommendation:

* keep the feature behind the kill-switch for now,
* do not roll it out globally yet,
* but continue advancing the architecture.

Next focus should be:

1. multi-industry corpus balancing,
2. industry-aware prompting,
3. per-domain reasoning profiles,
4. language-style grounding,
5. selective enablement by industry during tuning.

I do not view the audit as a failure.
I view it as confirmation that the reasoning architecture works and now needs commercial-language specialization.

The important thing is that we now have evidence that the system can genuinely elevate strategic reasoning quality while still remaining governed by deterministic infrastructure and reliability controls.
