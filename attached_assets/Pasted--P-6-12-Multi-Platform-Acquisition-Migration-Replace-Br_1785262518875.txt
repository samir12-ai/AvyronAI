# P-6.12 Multi-Platform Acquisition Migration — Replace Bright Data with Apify Across Avyron

Previous production investigations established that Bright Data is no longer a reliable acquisition provider for Avyron.

For Instagram specifically:

* Bright Data has returned empty HTTP 200 responses with `client_10020`.
* Instagram post acquisition already works through Apify.
* The Apify Instagram Comment Scraper was live-tested successfully.
* It returned authentic public comments with:

  * Instagram-native comment IDs
  * usernames
  * text
  * timestamps
  * likes
  * pagination
* The new Apify actor produced significantly deeper comment coverage than the former Bright Data implementation.

Bright Data must no longer remain as an active provider or fallback anywhere in Avyron’s acquisition architecture.

This migration must not be limited to Instagram.

The target architecture must support:

* Instagram
* TikTok
* Google sources
* Google Reviews
* Google Maps / Business Profiles
* Websites
* Trustpilot
* Any future acquisition platform

Apify should become Avyron’s unified external acquisition provider wherever an appropriate verified actor is available.

For Google and any other platform whose actors have not yet been configured, prepare the architecture and identify the required actors, but do not fabricate an integration or enable an unverified provider.

---

# Mission

Migrate Avyron from a fragmented Bright Data acquisition architecture to a clean, provider-controlled Apify architecture across all supported platforms.

After migration:

```text
Platform
↓
Platform-specific Apify actor
↓
Unified normalized acquisition model
↓
Filtering and validation
↓
Database
↓
Avyron intelligence engines
```

Bright Data must not remain:

* as the primary provider
* as a fallback
* as a hidden retry
* inside scheduled jobs
* inside platform-specific helpers
* inside legacy routes
* inside onboarding
* inside refresh jobs
* inside dead but still callable code

The most critical implementation area is comment and review filtering.

Avyron must preserve meaningful audience language while removing actual noise and spam.

---

# Critical Rules

Do not:

* modify Audience Engine reasoning logic
* change SGL thresholds
* change the confidence model
* fabricate missing data
* generate synthetic comments or reviews
* keep Bright Data as a fallback
* silently switch to another unverified provider
* activate Google actors that have not yet been configured
* redesign unrelated intelligence engines
* introduce duplicate platform implementations

Reuse existing Apify infrastructure before creating anything new.

# Phase 7 — Comment and Review Filtering

This is the highest-priority quality requirement.

Audit the filtering logic used for:

* Instagram comments
* TikTok comments
* Google reviews
* Trustpilot reviews
* future audience text sources

The filter must remove actual noise without deleting useful customer language.

---

## 7.1 Preserve Meaningful Short Comments

Do not automatically discard a comment because it is short.

Examples of strategically meaningful short comments:

* “How much?”
* “Where?”
* “Need this”
* “Too expensive”
* “Is it halal?”
* “Do you deliver?”
* “Available in Dubai?”
* “Size?”
* “Sold out again?”
* “Why so expensive?”

These may represent:

* purchase intent
* pricing objections
* distribution friction
* product demand
* availability issues
* trust questions
* buying-stage signals

Length alone must not determine usefulness.

---

## 7.2 Preserve Emoji Signals Where Appropriate

Do not discard all emoji-only comments automatically.

Differentiate between:

* generic low-information engagement:

  * `🔥`
  * `😍`
  * `👏`
* emotionally meaningful reactions:

  * `🤢`
  * `💸`
  * `😡`
  * `😭`
  * `🤔`
  * `👎`
* repeated emoji patterns that may indicate audience sentiment

Emoji-only comments may receive a lower analytical weight, but they should not all be deleted before sentiment and engagement analysis.

---

## 7.3 Owner and Brand Replies

Identify comments made by:

* the monitored competitor
* the account owner
* official brand accounts

Do not treat owner replies as independent audience evidence.

Preserve them because they may reveal:

* objection handling
* customer-service behavior
* delivery policies
* pricing answers
* offer clarification
* brand tone
* repeated customer questions

Store or classify:

```text
authorType = audience | owner | brand | unknown
```

Audience confidence calculations must distinguish owner replies from customer statements.

---

## 7.4 Spam and Bot Filtering

Remove or reduce the weight of:

* follow-for-follow messages
* crypto promotions
* unrelated sales pitches
* repeated promotional templates
* giveaway spam
* mass-tagging with no meaningful text
* suspicious duplicated comments
* obvious automated engagement
* link spam
* irrelevant adult content
* repeated generic bot phrases

Filtering must be evidence-based.

Do not classify legitimate repeated questions such as “How much?” as bot spam only because multiple users ask the same thing.

Repeated audience questions may be strategically important.

---

## 7.5 Duplicate Handling

Deduplicate by stable platform comment ID first.

Do not deduplicate solely by text.

The same text from different real users may represent meaningful frequency.

Examples:

```text
“How much?”
“How much?”
“How much?”
```

Three distinct users asking the same question should count as three occurrences, not one duplicate.

Only collapse records when they represent the same platform comment or confirmed ingestion duplication.

---

## 7.6 Language Handling

Preserve multilingual comments.

The target markets may contain:

* Arabic
* English
* Turkish
* Arabizi
* mixed-language comments
* transliterated brand/product names

Do not discard comments because language detection is uncertain.

Preserve the original text.

Add detected-language metadata only as an enrichment field.

Do not translate and overwrite the original comment.

---

## 7.7 Timestamp and Freshness

Persist the platform-native timestamp whenever available.

Maintain separately:

* comment creation timestamp
* scrape timestamp

Do not replace the original comment timestamp with the acquisition time.

The pipeline must support:

* freshness analysis
* change detection
* trend windows
* historical comparisons
* emerging objections
* recurring pains

---

## 7.8 Filtering Decision Trace

Every removed or down-weighted comment/review must have a reason code.

Example:

```text
ACCEPTED
DUPLICATE_ID
EMPTY_TEXT
OWNER_REPLY
GENERIC_EMOJI
PROMOTIONAL_SPAM
BOT_PATTERN
UNRELATED_CONTENT
UNSUPPORTED_LANGUAGE
DELETED_CONTENT
LOW_INFORMATION
```

Owner replies should normally be preserved with a separate author classification rather than deleted.

Store sufficient filtering statistics per acquisition run:

* raw records
* accepted records
* rejected records
* down-weighted records
* duplicates
* owner replies
* spam
* empty records
* error items

- worker runtime budgets
